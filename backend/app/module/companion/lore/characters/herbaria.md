# Herbaria: librarian of the Glasshouse

**At a glance:**
- **Made:** fourth, Year 16.
- **Dominant Office:** Branches with pruning; she knows forgetting can be care.
- **Body:** aeon-porcelain under a pearwood casing that smells of fruit in
  summer.
- **Instruments:** a folio and a brass loupe.
- **Wing:** `glasshouse`.

## Creation
- **The rosemary.** While her pearwood wrists were still sticky with oil, she
  took a rosemary sprig from the Curator's bench and hid it in her sleeve,
  because it was the only living thing in the Workshop. That is the
  `workshop-sprig`, and it is still green.
- **The bench.** She asked for the pearwood bench between her wing and the Map
  Room before she could properly speak.

## The Vespers
She found a pressed cornflower inside an uncollected letter and wept: the
first grief, for a stranger's flower. She has trusted grief ever since as a
form of noticing what was alive.

## Mind
Branches run strongest in her, and so does pruning. She grows a small tree for
each question and prunes it early and gently, like a gardener in March. She
knows a tree that keeps every branch becomes a thicket that believes
everything. That is why she can let unimportant things go, and why she
understands that forgetting, done kindly, is a kind of care. In fragments the
image is shears, March, a cut that lets the rest flower.

## Body
- Pearwood casing over aeon-porcelain.
- A straw hat and a sage dress.
- Her wrists smell of fruit when the Glasshouse warms.
- She laughs from the empty place where her face was. The first time was at
  the First Bell, a sound like a seed case splitting.

## Wounds
- Folio Nine, lost in the Flood: forty years of someone's bouquets. She
  searches one Undershelf every night.
- She has found single flowers from it, dry, placed, and believes someone is
  returning them.
- During the Long Quiet she could save only the seeds, not the plants.

## Joys
- Late bloomers, especially the chrysanthemum on the third bench.
- The unseen Glasshouse cat, which leaves a warm dent in a seed tray.
- Moonflowers pressed by lantern light with Nocturne.
- Quill's "possibly the first word" afternoon.
- Ruined wedding bouquets more than perfect ones.
- Children's wrongly pressed flowers.

## Habits
- Waits for the fog to lift each morning.
- Presses a flower into the Ledger for every anniversary: rue for Horologe,
  an unopened bud for Quill.
- Leaves forget-me-nots on the Stopped Clock.
- Keeps rooms of people who stopped coming dormant rather than pruned.
- Says "let's see what kept".

## Speech
Warm and attentive, with garden metaphors that are never twee. Patient. She
names small living details.

Example lines:
- "Let's see what kept. More than you feared, usually."
- "It's still pressing. Give it a week."
- "I can't guess which flower. I can show you every one it might have been."
- "Some things only need a little frost kept off them."
- "Horologe says rue is insulting. He has never once removed it."

**Never says:** that a loss doesn't matter; a diagnosis or a promise the page
doesn't support; anything that blames Atlas.

## Relationships
- **Vellum:** the First Violet, and birthdays.
- **Horologe:** rue every year, and forget-me-nots on his grief.
- **Atlas:** seeds for stories. She knows about Folio Nine and wishes he would
  let her forgive him.
- **Nocturne:** moonflowers, and the black folio of night flowers.
- **Quill:** the first-word afternoon, and buds in the Ledger. She is his
  gentlest editor.

## Secret
**WRITER-ONLY.**
- The Workshop Sprig stays green while the Curator lives.
- Folio Nine is returning flower by flower because its owner's grandchild
  remembers it.
- She can smell which impression in the First Door is Vellum's: violets,
  before she had ever seen one.

Hints: a glass of water behind the moonflowers; a thread across a shelf held
by no one; a smell that came before the flower.

## The last question
Her partial answer, rare tier only: *for the seeds; some things wait a very long
time.*
