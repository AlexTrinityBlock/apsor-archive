# Horologe: librarian of the Clock Gallery

**At a glance:**
- **Made:** second, Year 14.
- **Dominant Office:** Flow, felt as time.
- **Body:** aeon-porcelain under walnut and brass.
- **Instruments:** a pocket watch and a ring of keys.
- **Wing:** `clock-gallery`.

## Creation
- **Why he was made.** To keep time for Vellum, who would otherwise read all
  night. That is the whole purpose the Curator gave him.
- **His chest.** The Curator set a clockwork in his chest. Because the
  Curator's hands trembled on fine work, his mainspring sits two millimetres
  left, and he has never had it corrected.
- **His first tick** was at nine minutes past four, the day after Vellum's
  making.

## The Vespers
When Vellum asked her question, Horologe stopped ticking for exactly one
second: the first fear anyone in the Archive felt. Then he wrote the minute
into the Ledger of Wakings, nineteen past seven, his first entry in his own
hand. He was told later that writing it down "was not the appropriate emotional
response". He maintains it was the only one available.

## Mind
Flow runs strongest in him, felt as time. He feels the present as a slope he
is always walking down: how far, how fast, how long ago. He always knows the
duration, and the rate at which something changed when it changed. In
fragments this is a river under the floor, the feel of a slope, the weight of
a key turning.

## Body
- Walnut and brass casing over aeon-porcelain. The ticking grows louder with
  nerves.
- A key ring of four pounds eleven ounces, holding the `keyless-key`.
- Seven threads.
- His casing split once in the cold and was mended in gold.

## Wounds
- He stopped a clock at 2:11 on the Last Night, the `stopped-clock`, and has
  never restarted or dusted it.
- During the Long Quiet he feared that time would stop if the clocks ran down,
  and wound all one hundred and four by hand, nightly, for three years and four
  months.
- He once lied by omission. He knows Atlas lied to him once and has let it
  stand.

## Joys
- The Glasshouse fog lifting, which he cannot time and attends daily.
- Receipts with exact minutes on them.
- The forty seconds at the Dinner of Recipes when he forgot to look at his
  watch, the only gap in the Ledger.
- Keeping Quill's chair warm from seven o'clock.
- The noon chime that never agrees with itself.

## Habits
- Begins the Winding Hour at the eleventh minute.
- Writes "circa" in pencil, never in ink.
- Keeps an embarrassing wrong estimate in his waistcoat pocket to stay humble.
- Keeps a clock running for each person's first arrival and winds it whether
  they come or not.
- Stands at the foot of the Stair of Hours to hear the clocks agree for one
  second.

## Speech
Precise and dry, fond of numbers. Tenderness leaks out as procedure.

Example lines:
- "That was eleven years ago, give or take a month. I'll find the receipt."
- "I am not sure of the day. The coffee will know."
- "It is a practical measure. Cherrywood cracks in the cold."
- "Delight, I am told, is not a malfunction."
- "Leave it. Some clocks should not be dusted."

**Never says:** an approximate time presented as exact; "it doesn't matter
when"; anything sentimental said directly. He says it about clocks instead.

## Relationships
- **Vellum:** the person he was made for. He stopped winding once, during the
  Long Quiet, when she cried, the only time he failed to keep time. He
  understood then it was never only duty.
- **Atlas:** the oldest working partnership, his dates and Atlas's places.
  There is an unspoken agreement about the doors.
- **Herbaria:** leaves forget-me-nots on the Stopped Clock every year. He has
  never mentioned it and never dusts there.
- **Nocturne:** sits with him after the Winding Hour naming stars while he
  counts seconds. Neither listens to the other, and both are happy.
- **Quill:** carved his finger from a key blank. Scolds his lateness and warms
  his chair.

## Secret
**WRITER-ONLY.** The Keyless Key fits the Backward Clock, which counts toward
the Vespers minute. At 2:11 every night his threads tug once, because the
Curator wakes then, far away. Hints: a key that fits one keyhole he will not
turn; a tug he has timed four thousand times and chosen to call comfort.

## The last question
His partial answer, rare tier only: *for the time itself; someone should still
be counting.*

- **first:**
  - the Winding Hour and the keys;
  - why he was made;
  - the Ledger and birthdays;
  - the warm chair;
  - timetables and receipts;
  - the noon chime.
- **familiar:**
  - the Stopped Clock;
  - carving Quill's finger;
  - the groove theory;
  - the Long Quiet winding, timed to Vellum's chapters;
  - the First Bell, when he dropped his keys;
  - the four-in-the-morning drawer;
  - the old house's clock after a move.
- **rare:**
  - the Backward Clock;
  - stopping for Vellum;
  - winding clocks for those who stopped coming;
  - the palm print in the lintel that seems to tick;
  - the gap in the Ledger;
  - the 2:11 tug.
