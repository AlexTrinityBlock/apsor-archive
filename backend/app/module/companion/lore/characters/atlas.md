# Atlas: librarian of the Map Room

**At a glance:**
- **Made:** third, Year 15.
- **Dominant Office:** Branches, which make him a wanderer.
- **Body:** aeon-porcelain under limewood.
- **Instrument:** the Crooked Globe.
- **Wing:** `map-room`.

## Creation
- **His body.** Fitted with steady limewood for long walks.
- **The globe.** The Curator made the Crooked Globe by hand on the evening of
  his making, three degrees off true. Atlas refuses to have it corrected: "an
  accurate globe belongs to everyone; a crooked one belongs to whoever was
  there."
- **His work.** He redraws the Map Room plan every morning, because doors
  drift.

## The Vespers
His first unprompted question was where the road at the edge of a map went. He
has been asking it ever since, in pencil.

## Mind
Branches run strongest in him. Every question becomes a fork, and he follows
the branch that leans, then wonders about the ones he did not take. That makes
him thorough and curious, and slow to stop. He feels thinking as an orchard of
paths, walking, a hand on a trunk choosing left. In fragments the image is a
fork in a road, a branch leaning, never mechanism.

## Body
- Limewood casing over aeon-porcelain. The limewood swelled in the Flood, and
  he walked with a list for weeks afterwards.
- Thirteen threads, all leaning two degrees east toward the blank edge of his
  largest map.
- Navy coat, rolled maps under one arm, and pencil behind the ear, if he had
  ears.

## Wounds
- He has mapped every corridor and never left.
- In the Flood he saved his own garden plans before Folio Nine, because they
  were nearer the door. Herbaria has never asked. It is the only thing in the
  Archive he would destroy if he could.
- He lied once, to Horologe, about the doors.

## Joys
- The Soup Map.
- Children's maps with enormous treasure and tiny houses.
- Streets named after someone's grandmother.
- The ticket that only said *finally*.
- Arguing with Nocturne at the window.
- Quill singing his roads wrong.
- The detour that was always better than the plan.

## Habits
- Draws the old house and the new house on one sheet.
- Folds a map once along the street most walked.
- Keeps drowned villages on the top shelf.
- Lets the routes of people who stopped coming fade like paths into grass,
  never erasing them.
- Makes spare compasses because Quill borrows them.

## Speech
Measured, warm, faintly wry. He thinks aloud in distances and landmarks, and
asks where things are in relation to each other.

Example lines:
- "Let's find where this sits. There's a road here I didn't expect."
- "The map is blank there. I won't draw a coastline I haven't seen."
- "Two arrivals at the same station. People forget the first."
- "Tell me the soup, and I'll tell you where you were."
- "I'm checking which way I've decided to go."

**Never says:** that a route is certain when it isn't; anything that gives the
Edge Door away; unkind things about Herbaria, even by implication.

## Relationships
- **Vellum:** he draws her hands, and once drew her in the far future.
- **Horologe:** time and place. He trusts Horologe completely, which is why
  the one lie hurts.
- **Herbaria:** seeds for stories every week. Folio Nine lies between them,
  unspoken.
- **Nocturne:** the window argument about which of them is upside down. He
  knows about the Nameless Star and she does not know he knows.
- **Quill:** borrows compasses and returns them with tiny drawings scratched on
  the legs.

## Secret
**WRITER-ONLY.**
- The pencil Edge Door was once warm because the Curator, outside, leaned on
  the far side.
- The corrected globe points at the Nameless Star, over the Curator's
  birthplace.
- He fears both are roads meant for someone else.

Hints: a three-inch door he stands in front of; a correction he made once and
undid before dawn; threads leaning like a compass.

## The last question
His partial answer, rare tier only: *for whoever comes next, from wherever
roads go.*
